01 / THE NUMBERS

Understanding your numbers

A little perspective—not a payout statement.

How is the estimate calculated?

For supported Spotify views, included plays × $0.003 gives an illustrative recording-royalty estimate. The benchmark is Duetti’s 2024 Spotify sample average of $3 per 1,000 streams—not a fixed Spotify rate.

The global independent-catalog sample is not representative of every artist, market, plan, or period. Read the benchmark ↗

Does the artist receive this amount?

No. The estimate is associated with recordings, not confirmed earnings or artist take-home. Spotify uses streamshare rather than a fixed per-play payment.

Label, distributor, and collaborator agreements affect what artists receive. One listener’s history cannot establish track eligibility or platform-wide thresholds. Spotify’s royalties guide ↗

Which plays are included?

Spotify imports include valid, dated music-track records with artist and title metadata and at least 30 seconds played. Short plays, unsupported content, invalid records, and records outside your selected dates are excluded.

Matching playback occurrences are deduplicated across imports. Artist attribution follows the file; collaborator splits and royalty eligibility are not inferred. Manual counts are self-reported.

Your current view’s audit

Why are some numbers unavailable?

CSV events are always unpriced. Last.fm scrobbles do not establish a streaming service or royalties; a Spotify benchmark scenario requires a separate opt-in and source declaration. Non-Spotify manual counts are also unpriced.

Missing duration stays unknown. Hourly patterns require precise timestamps and use UTC. Comparisons describe the supplied records: gaps do not prove zero listening, and being new in a period does not mean an artist was newly discovered.

What does a receipt contain?

A receipt captures highlights of the selected view, with its source labels and limitations. Sample receipts are fictional, and self-reported or unpriced views stay labeled that way.

Images, recap cards, and summary CSVs are keepsakes or summaries—not full-history backups. Make your receipt →

02 / YOUR HISTORY

Using Payola

Local first. An account only if you want one.

Which files can I import?

Use Spotify Extended Streaming History audio JSON or ZIP files, or map a CSV of listening events. CSV supports self-reported Spotify, Apple Music, and YouTube Music sources but is always unpriced. You can also enter artist play counts manually.

Native Apple archive auto-detection is not supported. A limited YouTube activity JSON/ZIP route accepts only explicitly music-marked events with channel metadata; general watch history is excluded. Playlists, liked songs, and libraries do not establish plays. Optional Last.fm and the limited Apple recent-track preview depend on deployment configuration. Add listening →

What if an import fails or I cancel?

Your open history stays unchanged until you confirm the review. Only successful files are staged; canceling discards that pending selection. Add checks matching occurrences across files. Replace starts a new history only after confirmation.

Check the file feedback and column mapping, try a smaller batch, or unzip Spotify files first. Date-only CSVs cannot distinguish all listening events reliably.

Where does my data go?

File parsing stays on your device. Signing in connects to Firebase/Google, but never uploads listening automatically. A confirmed history save uploads normalized records, dates, and source declarations—not original files.

A separately confirmed collection save uploads the receipt summary you review, including its title, private note, totals, disclosures, theme, and up to three artist highlights. It does not upload raw listening events.

Optional Last.fm requests send your username and requested dates through this server to Last.fm. Hosting infrastructure may log requests. There are no analytics, external fonts, or artist-image requests.

How do I keep or restore my history?

Guest history stays in this tab and is lost on reload or clearing. Download a full session file to keep it, then restore that file later. Session files contain listening history—keep them private.

Verified accounts can explicitly save or load one full-history cloud snapshot, up to 4 MiB. The separate receipt collection holds up to 20 summaries with titles and notes. Saving a receipt is not a history backup, and there is no autosave. Local backups → Account & collection →

What stays saved on this device?

Listening history is not automatically persisted. You can explicitly remember a Last.fm username, a Firebase sign-in, or supported import and receipt preferences. Downloaded files remain wherever you save them.

Use Forget saved username or sign out to remove the corresponding remembered choice. Sign-out can also clear the open local history when selected. Receipt drafts stay only in tab memory and are discarded on reload, sign-out, or switching away from an existing account.

How do accounts and deletion work?

Payola Reciept and Job Tracker share a login, not listening and job data. Password changes affect both apps. Shared-account deletion is not offered here.

Delete collection receipts and the full-history cloud snapshot separately. These actions do not delete your jobs, shared login, or open local view. Empty slots and version markers remain for conflict safety.

If removing your login through Job Tracker, delete Payola cloud data first: cleanup is not automatic. No support address is currently published; never share passwords, tokens, or raw history in public reports.
